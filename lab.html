Future Gadget Laboratory

Second floor, above the Braun Tube Workshop, Akihabara. Rintaro Okabe founded it in mid-March 2010, and by August it has eight members, each with a number and each tied to a world line. Point at a member, or tab to one, and the meter in the bar reads their line.

This page spoils the whole story.

Lab members 001-008

Numbered in the order they joined. The last column is the world line each one is tied to: the line their own D-mail made, or the one where their story turns.

Glossary

Thirty terms, in the order the story needs them. Everything here is our own summary.

World line
One self-consistent history of the universe. Only one is active at a time.
Divergence
The number that names a world line: its offset from 0.000000. Changes past the sixth decimal place do not register.
Attractor field
A bundle of world lines that converge on the same key events. Alpha (0.xxxxxx): Mayuri dies and SERN rules. Beta (1.xxxxxx): Kurisu dies and a world war follows.
Convergence
Fate inside a field. Change the details and the outcome still arrives.
1% barrier
The divergence you have to cross to get from the Alpha field to the Beta field.
Steins Gate world line
1.048596. It lies between the two fields and belongs to neither. Okabe named it.
Reading Steiner
Okabe's ability to keep his memories across a shift. It comes as vertigo, and for a moment his sight drains to monochrome.
D-mail
Short for "DeLorean Mail": a text message sent into the past through the PhoneWave. It carries at most 36 bytes.
PhoneWave (name subject to change)
Future Gadget #8: a microwave switched on by calling a phone. It makes tiny black holes.
Gelbana
A banana the PhoneWave has turned into green gel. The first clue that something is wrong with the microwave.
42-inch CRT
Mr. Braun's giant Braun-tube television, one floor below the lab. Its cathode rays steady the black holes into rotating Kerr black holes.
Time Leap Machine
Kurisu's upgrade. It compresses your memories and sends them into your own brain, up to 48 hours back, received through a phone.
Divergence Meter
Eight Nixie tubes built by a future Okabe to show the current divergence. The meter on this site is an original drawing, not the prop.
Nixie tube
A neon display tube from the 1950s. The digits 0 to 9 are wire cathodes stacked front to back and lit at about 170 volts.
El Psy Kongroo
Okabe's sign-off on his pretend phone calls to "the Organization". The official spelling is Kongroo; subtitles often write Congroo.
Hououin Kyouma
Okabe's mad-scientist alter ego, and what he calls his true name. It began as a way to pull a grieving Mayuri back.
The Organization
The agency Okabe pretends is hunting him. It turns out to be real, and it is SERN.
The choice of Steins Gate
Okabe's catchphrase for whatever happens, which he admits means nothing. By the end it names a real choice.
Lab Mem
A numbered member of the lab. 001 to 008 appear in Steins;Gate; 009 to 011 join in Steins;Gate 0.
OSHMKUFA 2010
The lab-mem badge: the members' initials in joining order, and the year the lab was founded.
Operation Urd, Verthandi, Skuld
Okabe's operations, named after the Norns of past, present and future: the D-mail tests, the time leaps and the undoing, and the final rescue.
SERN
A parody of CERN that secretly researches time travel with its collider.
Z Program and jellymen
SERN's human time-travel experiments, whose subjects end up as gel.
Rounders
SERN's mercenary strike force. Their orders come from FB, who is Mr. Braun.
ECHELON
The real signals-intelligence network. In the story, it is how SERN logged the first D-mail.
IBN 5100
A parody of the IBM 5100 of 1975, and the only machine that can read SERN's legacy code.
John Titor
In real life, a poster from 2000 who claimed to come from 2036 and to be looking for an IBM 5100. In the story, Suzuha's handle.
Metal Upa
The rare metal version of Upa, a round mascot. It is Mayuri's capsule prize, and it decides whether the thesis survives.
Tutturu
Mayuri's sing-song greeting. She calls herself Mayushii.
@channel
The story's version of the 2channel textboard, where John Titor posts.

Akihabara, fiction and real

Where the story happens, and the real places it borrows from. Several of them are homes, shrines and working streets: visit quietly.
