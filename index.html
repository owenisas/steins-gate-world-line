Steins;Gate in four ideas

Akihabara, summer 2010. Rintaro Okabe, eighteen and a self-declared mad scientist, runs a two-room "laboratory" with his friends. Their latest gadget, a microwave wired to a phone, turns out to send text messages into the past.

A one-minute read. Nothing here goes past episode 5.

D-mail

A text message sent into the past through that microwave, the PhoneWave. It carries at most 36 bytes. Whoever receives it acts on it, and history quietly rearranges itself around the change.

Write one in the composer

World lines

History is a bundle of threads, and each thread is one complete version of events. A D-mail moves the whole world onto another thread, where the past was always different.

Point at a thread below.

The Divergence Meter

A number names the thread you are on, to six decimal places. The story opens on 1.130426. One text message later, the meter reads 0.571024.

Reading Steiner

When the world line shifts, everyone's memories shift with it. Only Okabe keeps the old ones, through a grey moment of vertigo. He calls it Reading Steiner, and it makes him the only witness.

At Radio Kaikan, Kurisu Makise lies in a pool of blood.

July 28, 2010. Rintaro Okabe, eighteen, a first-year student who introduces himself as the mad scientist Hououin Kyouma, takes his childhood friend Mayuri Shiina to Dr. Nakabachi's time-travel press conference at the Radio Kaikan building in Akihabara.

Inside, he finds a young researcher, Kurisu Makise, lying in a pool of blood, and texts his friend Daru what he saw.

Then the world blinks. The crowds are gone. A "satellite" has crashed into the building, and the conference has been cancelled. Daru says the text reached him a week before Okabe sent it. It is the first D-mail. The world line has changed, and its divergence reads 0.571024 instead of 1.130426.

The Future Gadget Laboratory rents the second floor of a tired building above Mr. Braun's CRT shop. Its eighth invention is a microwave you switch on by calling the phone wired into it. It only works while the 42-inch CRT downstairs is switched on, and what it actually does is turn bananas into green gel. The lab calls them gelbanas.

Kurisu Makise is alive after all. A heated debate with Okabe interests her enough to follow him to the lab. Online, a poster calling himself John Titor haunts @channel. The lab breaks into SERN's network, finds files on a "Z Program" and on "jellymen", and learns that SERN's old code can only be read on an IBN 5100. One of those is kept at Yanabayashi Shrine. It is still a comedy.

Once the lab accepts that the PhoneWave sends text into the past, Okabe names the experiments Operation Urd, after the Norn of what was. A D-mail holds at most 36 bytes, the PhoneWave's timer sets how many whole hours back it lands, and every one they send grants somebody a wish.

Time leap

August 13

Kurisu turns the PhoneWave into a Time Leap Machine, and on August 13 armed Rounders storm the lab and kill Mayuri.

Read the full chapter

Kurisu rebuilds the PhoneWave into a Time Leap Machine, the first stage of what Okabe files as Operation Verthandi. It does not send text. It compresses a person's memories and plays them into that person's own brain, received through a phone at the ear, at most 48 hours back.

On August 13 the lab throws a party. Armed Rounders, SERN's strike force, storm the room to take the machine, and Moeka is leading them. Mayuri is shot dead. Okabe puts on the headset and leaps.

This is the hinge. Everything above this line was a comedy.

The first leap lands at 4:56 pm on August 13. Mayuri dies anyway. Okabe leaps again, and again: a gunshot, a train, a car. Inside the Alpha attractor field the details can change but the outcome arrives, and on every Alpha line she dies in mid-to-late August 2010.

Suzuha Amane, the part-timer from the CRT shop, tells him she is John Titor, a fighter from a 2036 ruled by SERN. Her Divergence Meter reads 0.337187. The only way out is across the 1% barrier into the Beta field. She finds her father, who turns out to be Daru, and leaves for 1975.

To start the climb, Okabe cancels his own D-mail. On 0.409431 Suzuha died in 2000, and Mr. Braun has kept her meter. He hands it to Okabe on August 14.

Operation Skuld

From August 21, Okabe rides back to July 28 to save Kurisu without changing a thing his younger self saw.

by Okabe, from his own 2025

World line 1.130205 to 1.048596

August 21. A Beta-line Suzuha lands a working time machine on the Radio Kaikan roof. On this line, she says, Kurisu's death and her father's theft of her thesis lead to World War III. Okabe rides back to July 28 and, in the struggle with Nakabachi, stabs Kurisu himself by accident. Convergence again.

Then a garbled video D-mail arrives from Okabe's own future. The plan: do not change what the younger Okabe saw, change what happened. Deceive the world. He calls it Operation Skuld.

He swaps Mayuri's Metal Upa for a plastic one, so nothing sets off the metal detector and the thesis burns in the plane's hold. He goads Nakabachi into stabbing him instead, and leaves the unconscious Kurisu lying in his blood for his younger self to find. The meter reads 1.048596.

07 The choice09 Steins Gate

Chapter 09 Steins Gate

Nobody has to die.

On world line 1.048596 both Mayuri and Kurisu live, and weeks later Okabe meets her again, by chance, on an Akihabara street.

Read the full chapter

On 1.048596 nobody has to die. Not Mayuri, not Kurisu. There is no SERN dystopia and no world war. The thesis is ash, and Nakabachi is detained by the Russian authorities. The line belongs to neither attractor field, and Okabe gives it a name: Steins Gate.

He wakes in hospital with a stab wound. Weeks later, on an Akihabara street, he and Kurisu meet by chance, and she half remembers a summer that did not happen here. A world chosen, not granted: the choice of Steins Gate.

D-mail

A text message sent into the past through the lab's PhoneWave. It can carry at most 36 bytes.

PhoneWave (name subject to change)

Future Gadget #8: a microwave you switch on by calling the phone wired into it. By accident, it can send texts into the past.

SERN

The story's version of CERN, the particle-physics lab near Geneva. Rumours online say it studies time travel in secret.

IBN 5100

The story's version of IBM's 1975 portable computer. It can read old code that modern machines cannot.

John Titor

A poster on @channel who claims to come from the year 2036. The name is borrowed from a real internet legend of 2000.

Reading Steiner

Okabe's name for keeping his memories when the world line changes. Everyone else remembers only the new history.

World line

One complete version of history. A D-mail can move the whole world onto another one.

Divergence

The number that names a world line, read to six decimal places.

Divergence Meter

Eight Nixie tubes that show the divergence of the world line you are on.

Attractor field

A bundle of world lines that all lead to the same key events, however the details change on the way.

Time Leap Machine

A rebuilt PhoneWave that sends a person's memories, not text, up to 48 hours into their own past.

Rounders

SERN's armed strike force.

Operation Urd

Okabe's name for the D-mail experiments, after Urd, the Norn of what was.

Operation Verthandi
